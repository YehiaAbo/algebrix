        Manual

        1. Aim, then act

        You never edit characters. Aim at a piece with the arrows (or Space and the letter over it), then press a key: m moves it across the equals sign the right way, s simplifies, d distributes.

        2. Both sides at once

        = - . / add, subtract, multiply or divide both sides. Type what, watch the live preview, press Enter. No Shift needed.

        3. Nothing breaks

        Every move is checked by plugging in numbers. A move that would change the answers resists, and the reason shows in the bottom bar. Dividing by something that could be zero adds a condition to the rail.

        4. Simplify when you want

        Nothing tidies itself. A dashed box marks what could simplify; press s to take it, or leave it if you want that form.

        5. Hold Tab for algebra

        Factor, expand, combine fractions, clear denominators, split into cases, solve for a variable (i), substitute from another equation (1–9). Arrows browse; release Tab (or tap it again) to apply. e on its own solves straight away.

        6. Hold f for functions

        Put a function on both sides, or press i to undo the outer one. ← → pick a family (powers, roots, exp and log, trig), ↓ steps in, release f to apply.

        7. Many equations

        The equation you work on gets the room; the others sit open on the shelf across the top, each saying what it shares with this one (click one to swap). n adds one; Alt + ← → or 1–9 move between them. Aim at a variable and the equations that share it light up: hold Tab + their number to bring it in. Extraneous answers are flagged.

        8. The graph

        One variable: both sides as curves, and the answers are where they cross. Two: the solution curve, or the shaded region for an inequality. Three: the solution surface in 3D. Drag, scroll, double-click to reset.

        9. Pick part of a side

        Drag a box with the mouse around the pieces you want, or hold Ctrl and tap ← → to grow it left or right. 3 + 5 out of 2x + 3 + 5 + y works like any single piece: s, m, and the rest.

        10. More than equations

        Type x^2 − 4 < 0 for an inequality (moves flip the sign when they must; e solves it with a sign chart). Type just an expression to rewrite it. An identity like (a+b)^2 = a^2+2ab+b^2 becomes a proof. f(x) = x^2 + 1 defines a function.

        11. Your work

        w shows every workspace as a card: open, rename, copy, or start a new one. Shift+R twice puts every equation back at its start (r replays your steps).

        12. Hold C for calculus

        Type d/dx x^2 sin x or lim x->0 sin(x)/x; while you type, a grey □ marks what is still to come (Enter waits until every □ is filled). C opens in sections: Derivatives, Limits, Integrals, Series, Several variables, Differential equations, Vector fields. ← → pick a section (a dot marks the ones that apply), ↓ steps in, Enter applies, or press an item's key at once. On any expression, l wraps it in a limit and a in a definite integral: type where x goes, or the bounds like 0, pi. Hold C: d takes the next derivative rule (its name shows on the key), p plugs in, l is L'Hôpital. On a function, d adds f′ as a new equation and m finds where the slope is 0; on an equation, it differentiates both sides and y′ appears. e works all of it out. Integrals too: type int x^2 dx or int_0^1 x^2 dx, then i integrates, u substitutes (u = … goes on the shelf), b is by parts, p plugs in the bounds. Series: sum_{n=0}^∞ (1/2)^n, with g geometric and r the ratio test; t gives a Taylor polynomial. Two letters, like x^2 y + y^3: d is ∂/∂x (aim at y for ∂/∂y), n the gradient ∇, m sets every partial to 0, and k is Lagrange with a constraint you typed as another equation. A vector equation splits into one equation per entry with e. Type vectors as (1, 2) and double integrals as int_0^1 int_0^2 x y dy dx. Differential equations: type y' = x y, y' + 2y = e^x, y'' - 3y' + 2y = 0 or with a right side like y'' + y = sin(x) (undetermined coefficients). The graph shows a slope field (hover to trace a solution), the shaded area of a definite integral, and where a limit heads. s separates, f is the integrating factor, r the characteristic equation, g the general solution, and e picks the method. On a field like (-y, x, 0), v is the divergence and w the curl.

        13. Odd notation

        While you type, part of a word (int, lim, alpha, or a LaTeX \sum) brings up cards with the notation it could mean: integrals single to triple, sums and products, one-sided limits, ∂, ∇, Greek letters and more. ↓ ↑ pick one, Tab takes it and puts the caret where you type next. Stack integrals freely: int int x y dx dy. Products: prod_{k=1}^5 k. A grey □ marks what is still missing.

        13. Make it yours

        , opens settings: theme, look (try Pixel), math size, speed, sound pack (try Blocks). z or the ⚡ at the bottom switches flash mode.
